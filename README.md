Scanner Methodology

1. Core Trading Philosophy
The central philosophy of this trading system is to enter the market only when the statistical odds are in your favor. This is achieved by identifying "high probability conditions," which are defined as moments of market volatility. Volatility presents a dual opportunity: a potential for quick profits but also a higher risk. To manage this risk, traders should consider using smaller position sizes and wider stop-losses.

A high-probability setup requires a confluence of factors, including the overall market trend, current volatility, and liquidity. The system uses technical analysis to pinpoint precise entry and exit points, reducing the influence of emotions.

2. The Daily Trading Setup Checklist
The First Candle Rule: Identify the opening market range to set the directional bias for the day.
Identifying High Probability Conditions: Look for the confluence of signals that indicate a high-probability setup.
Entry, Stop Loss, Target: Define and stick to your precise entry, stop-loss, and profit target levels.
Backtest: Validate the strategy against historical data to ensure its effectiveness.
3. The "First Candle Rule" Breakout Strategy
This strategy is designed to make consistent profits by trading only from 9:30 a.m. to 11:00 a.m. Eastern time. It works on all markets and uses the first candle of the day to determine trade direction, entry, and stop-loss levels.

30-Minute Framework:
Draw the high and low of the first 30-minute candle (9:30-10:00 a.m.).
Switch to the 5-minute chart and wait for the price to break either the high or the low.
A breakout is confirmed by a retest of the broken level or the formation of a Fair Value Gap (FVG).
5-Minute Framework:
Draw the high and low of the first 5-minute candle (9:30-9:35 a.m.).
Switch to the 1-minute chart and wait for a break of these levels.
Confirmation is the same: a retest or an FVG confirms a high-probability entry.
Key Execution Rules:
Stop-Loss Placement: Stop-loss should be set at the base of the candle bodies, not the wicks, for a more robust level.
Target: The strategy consistently aims for a fixed 2:1 risk-to-reward ratio.
